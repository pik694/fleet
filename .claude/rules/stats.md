# Local stats screen

- Stats (`i` / `Ctrl+K` → "Your Stats"; `internal/stats` + `internal/ui/stats_*.go`): **local-only** personal analytics in `~/.config/fleet/stats.db` (its own file, so "reset my stats" is deleting it; WAL). **Scope is fleet sessions only** — turns are backfilled from the transcripts of sessions fleet launched (joined via every Claude id a session has had, so a `/clear` rotation doesn't orphan history), never all Claude usage on the machine. **Forks must not count their parent twice**, which took three guards: a turn that starts before its fleet session's `created_at` is dropped at load (a `--fork-session` transcript copies the parent's history); a Claude id maps to its **first** owner (`claude_ids` is insert-or-ignore, because a fork's first SessionStart reports the *parent's* id and a last-writer-wins upsert handed the parent's transcript to the fork); and the scan reads **one file per Claude id**, the largest (a fork into another cwd stages a frozen copy of the parent's transcript under the parent's id). The in-fleet half comes from **recorder tees**: `recordStatus` beside every persisted status change (`updateAndPersistStatus` on the worker, suspend, resume, restart, `flagUnread`), `RecordAttach` in the attach callback, `NoteSession` on load/create/adopt and on a new Claude id, and `recordStats(stats.Action*)` for fleet actions — `logAction` maps through `statsActionFor`, a **fixed table read by action name only** (unmapped actions record nothing); actions logged *before* their own guard runs (quick approve, mark unread, open PR, copy PR link, open editor) are deliberately absent and record **where they succeed** — the `quickApproveMsg`/`openPRMsg`/`copyPRLinkMsg`/`openEditorMsg` handlers on `err == nil`, and `markUnreadSelected` past its guards (not `flagUnread`, which snooze expiry also calls) — or a `Y` on a running session counted as an approve, plus direct calls at Space/`P`/slot jumps (through `recordStatsJump`: counted only when the jump **lands on a different row** than the cursor started on, and a press within `statsRepeatWindow` (300ms) of the previous press of the same key — counted or not, so a held key is **one** jump however long it's held — continues that burst; terminal auto-repeat once turned a held Space into 942 "jumps" in a day), palette/help opens, rename, confirmed deletes, session and worktree creation. The recorder never blocks (drop-on-full queue, one writer goroutine) and every method is nil-safe, so a stats.db that fails to open costs only the feature. **Privacy line**: stats.db holds enums, ids, numbers, timestamps, model names and repo *base* names — never prompts, titles, message text, tool input or paths (a transcript is keyed by a hash of its realpath, since the path spells out the home dir and the project's cwd). Opening Stats is **not** recorded: pressing `i` must not inflate the numbers it opens. The first-run screen is chosen on **content, not coverage** (`statsReportEmpty`: no turn, no measured reply, no active day in the heatmap year) — status changes start the recording clock and loading the sidebar notes every session before the user has done anything. The transcript scan runs on a **`tea.Cmd` goroutine of its own**, never the status worker (whose 90s stall budget is spent on git/`gh`) and never `Update`: 10s after launch and on every Stats open (incremental by byte offset, ~20ms warm), progress throttled to ~10/s and skipped during an attach (`h.send` is a rendezvous the attach suspends). Reports and recaps compute off-goroutine after a `Flush`, guarded per range by a generation counter. The **"Your week" badge** is the only push surface: after each scan, and on the tick once the week rolls over (`maybeRecapNewWeek` — instances run for days), the last full Mon–Sun week's recap is computed, and if it has cards and its Monday ≠ `stats_recap_seen_week` the badge takes the header's top-right corner (hidden while a modal is open), sized to the room the breadcrumb leaves — it drops `· press i`, then itself, rather than overprint a title. It advertises `i`, so `i` with the badge up opens Stats **with the recap on top**; opening the recap marks the week seen. Recap cards copy with `c` through `tmux.CopyToClipboard`, **leaving out the lines that name repos** (`RecapCard.RepoLines`) unless `r` is toggled — repo and worktree names often carry client or ticket names, and a card is copied to be shared. The concurrency curve plots each bucket's **peak**, not its mean: a 6-hour bucket's mean sits under 1 and never reaches the peak the hero reports. **Layout is a card grid** (`statsGrid`): rounded `ColorBorder` cards with dim titles inset in the top border tile the full width (uncapped — a capped grid floated inside a full-width frame whose title and hints no longer shared its edges), columns pack greedily and wrap by width (a last row left holding one card steals the previous row's last one, rather than stretch a lone card across the screen), cards in a row share a height with a short stack's slack **split evenly** between its cards, and a card with no data is **left out** rather than drawn empty (hero tiles keep an honest `—`). **No card should be mostly blank**, which took three rules: which cards share a row under the chart is **chosen, not fixed** — `statsLowerLayouts` offers a handful of arrangements (everything in one row; records and fun facts stacked or side by side; the notes in a row of their own) and `statsBestGrid` keeps the one stranding the fewest blank card cells, counting rows under a short body, columns beside a narrow text body (records and fun facts are capped near their natural width so spare columns go to bar cards) and half of a bar card's columns past `fullW`, **plus a per-card penalty** for blank rows past `statsMaxBlankShare` (30%) of that card's body — summed cells alone never saw one hollow card, and let a one-row layout strand half of fleet actions under a tall records + facts stack; height is only a tiebreak (`cw/32` a line) until it shortens the chart and dear once it scrolls — at `cw/8` it outvoted the waste it was meant to break ties on (`TestStatsNoCardIsMostlyBlank`, measured off the rendered frame); lists stretched by their row **re-render to fill it** (`statsCardSpec.fit`) — fleet actions show the top five and a dim `+N more`, or as many as the row holds, and fun facts open a blank line between facts when they all still fit; and the **agents card exists only when it compares** (≥2 agents with turn data), otherwise the agent split rides a dim line under the repos, since one agent drew "Claude 100%" in a tall empty box. `statsReportLines` picks the richest style that fits — big hero digits + card padding, then padding, then neither — and grows the concurrency chart into the rows left **up to `statsChartMaxRows` (10)**: taller, a sparse week became a wall of thin spikes. Rows a tall screen still has spare become air, not stretched cards (`statsBalance`): the page is **top-anchored** — it starts right under the range tabs, spare rows open **at most one** blank row between bands, and the rest fall below the page — with the **footnote attached one row under the grid** (`TestStatsViewBalancesSpareRows`). Centring floated a short page mid-screen with a band of empty rows between the tabs and the first card; pinning the footnote to the bottom like a status line left 7–12 empty rows between the cards and their footnote at 250×62. **The range picker is a tab row pinned at the top of the page** (`rangeTabs`: `7 days   30 days   all time · Sep 24 – Sep 30`, the window dim beside it), the active tab a **mode** (`ModeOn`, never filled); `1`/`2`/`3` pick a range directly, `[` `]` / `←→` / `h l` cycle. It replaced a `‹ 7d 30d all ›` chip inset in the frame's top-right border that nobody noticed (a desktop notification covered it in the reporter's screenshot). A blank row under the tabs is spent only when the page fits without scrolling (`tabRows`). The window moved off the footnote onto the tabs. The scan's progress line rides under the footnote. The plot stops **one column short** of the card's text edge, or the bar for the hour in progress ran into the right border. The chart never snaps to a multiple of the peak, since eighth-block tops step cleanly at any height. The concurrency chart is solid block columns (`statsBlockChart`, never braille — Ghostty renders braille as sparse separate dots) laid on **equal-width slots** (`statsChartSlots`: with fewer than two samples per column every slot is ≥2 columns, since mixing 1- and 2-sample columns drew bursts as 1-column slivers and gaps), over a dim `─` baseline, with `┈` gridlines only when a level spans ≥3 rows. The heatmap is always two columns per week (one column reads as stripes), shows only weeks with data, and is sized to share a row with repos/agents when ≥16 weeks fit there. A day's level is the **higher of two quartile levels**, one over fleet actions and one over agent time (`statsDayLevels`): turns are backfilled from transcripts months before fleet records its first action, and scoring by actions alone once any day had one drew every backfilled day as a zero dot. Only days before the **earliest** data source (`TurnsSince` or `RecordingSince`) are blank. Every cell is the same `■` — a zero day a border-tone square, today a text-colour one, rows labelled `M T W T F S S`: zero days drawn as `·` read as static, and a `▣`/`□` today marker is East-Asian-Ambiguous, which Ghostty draws two columns wide, shearing the grid and eating the legend's space (`■` renders one column there). Charts are coloured with `StatsRamp` (built in `ApplyPalette`: step 0 is the border tone, the data steps start ~40% of the way to the accent so the lowest band doesn't read as chrome), the one sanctioned exception to "colour means status" (`docs/design-system.md` §5); concurrency uses three far-apart classes (solo / 2 / 3+). Under the chart, one line splits busy time into those classes (`statsShareLines`): a `when agents were busy` caption, a short bar (≤40 cols) and the shares **named in their segments' shades** — a full-width bar with a swatch legend and no caption read as an unexplained stripe, or as part of the chart's axis. Hero numbers are the only accent-coloured figures (`statsFigure`, units dim); when every tile in the row fits them and the page doesn't scroll they render in a 3-row half-block font (`statsBigFigure` — `▀ ▄ █` are solid cells with no seams, unlike box-drawing strokes), as does the recap's figure, **centred in the tile** (hugging the left edge left a wide tile mostly blank). **Units are glyphs in that font too** (`h m s ×`, dim), shaped never to pass for a digit (`s` is the round S, not the square 5), and a space in the value opens a three-column gap: drawn as ordinary letters beside three-row digits they shrank to subscripts, and `21h 00m` read as `2100`. Two deliberate exceptions: a **thousands comma is dropped** (`$1,412` draws as `1412`) — any one-pixel mark at that size read as a decimal point, including a comma half a row below the period — and **`$` is a small dim superscript** on the top row, as a price tag sets it, since six pixel rows drew it as a blob. A value holding a character the font lacks falls back to the one-line figure. Below the width that holds five full tiles the heroes collapse to a one-row strip (value only, short labels) instead of wrapping 3 + 2. The laid-out lines are cached per (size, range, data generation): `clampScroll` runs on every key. The API-price dollar figure is a fun fact about tokens you didn't pay for on a subscription, not a bill; it lives in the hero row only. **Fun facts that compare or rank must be earned by the data**: they wait for `minRecordedDays` (7) local days of **recording** (`recordedDays`: `recording_since` through today) whatever their samples say — a first weekend can clear every per-bucket gate with one burst — and then a comparison needs `minCompareSamples` (10) in **each** bucket it compares and a pattern must span `minFactDays` (3) distinct days (`replySpeedFact`, `busiestHourFact`, the not-attached share); the busiest hour must also hold time from `minFactDays` days of its own and beat the runner-up by `busiestHourMargin` (20%), or a near-tie crowned whichever hour came first — `MinSamples` (5) is enough to show a median, not to claim two buckets differ, and one afternoon of first-day recording produced "your replies get quicker when 2+ other agents are busy". Records only — no badges, achievements or personas.
